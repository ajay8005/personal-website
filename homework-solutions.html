STAT 151A: Linear Modelling (Theory & Applications)

STAT 151A at UC Berkeley focuses on modern statistical inference and linear modeling, emphasizing both theoretical foundations and practical applications.

Fundamentals
  • Linear Algebra: Matrix formulation of the General Linear Model and OLS estimation.
  • Inference: Hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, and the Gauss-Markov theorem.
  • Diagnostics: Model assessment, residual analysis, and identifying high-leverage points.
Advanced Inference
  • Regularization: Shrinkage methods including Ridge regression, LASSO, and Elastic Net.
  • Extensions: Generalized Linear Models (GLMs) for non-normal response variables.
  • Robustness: Outlier detection, influential observations, and robust regression techniques.

Students learn to implement and interpret these models, assess assumptions, and address common pitfalls such as confounding, omitted variables, and high-leverage points.

STAT 154/254: Modern Statistical Prediction & Machine Learning

STAT 154/254 at UC Berkeley covers a comprehensive set of foundational and advanced machine learning topics, blending statistical learning ideas with optimization and computation.

Basic Topics
  • Tasks: Regression, classification, dimension reduction, and clustering.
  • Algorithms: Solving linear systems, gradient descent, Newton’s method, power iteration for eigenvalue problems, and EM algorithms.
  • Others: Kernel methods, regularization, sample splitting, resampling methods, and cross-validation.
Advanced Topics
  • Statistical learning theory and optimization theory.
  • Bagging and boosting, tree-based models, neural networks, and Bayesian models.
  • Online learning and bandit problems.

The course emphasizes both mathematical understanding and algorithmic structure, with topics ranging from solving linear systems to modern predictive modeling.
